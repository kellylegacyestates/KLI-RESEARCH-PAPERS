# Kelly Legacy Institute
## Research Standard

**Repository:** KLI-RESEARCH-PAPERS  
**Institutional Owner:** Kelly Legacy Institute  
**Administrative Governance:** Office of the Fiduciary  

## 1. Governing Method

KLI research shall apply:

**Assertion → Authority → Verification → Qualification → Citation**

No material proposition should be published solely because it appears plausible.

## 2. Authority Hierarchy

### Tier I — Primary Authority and Official Records

Includes:

- constitutions;
- statutes;
- regulations;
- judicial opinions;
- official agency decisions;
- Federal Register materials;
- administrative dockets;
- administrative records;
- authenticated government records.

### Tier II — Highly Authoritative Secondary Sources

Includes:

- Restatements;
- Uniform Law Commission materials;
- recognized legal treatises;
- law reviews;
- peer-reviewed scholarship;
- established institutional research.

### Tier III — Professional and Analytical Sources

Includes:

- professional journals;
- recognized legal analysis;
- authoritative technical documentation;
- established research organizations.

### Tier IV — General Secondary Sources

Includes:

- textbooks;
- encyclopedic references;
- mainstream reporting;
- educational websites.

### Tier V — Research Leads Only

Includes:

- social media;
- videos;
- podcasts;
- forums;
- anonymous commentary;
- unverified AI output.

Tier V materials may generate a research lead but shall not, standing alone, establish a material institutional proposition.

## 3. Administrative-Law Research Sequence

For federal administrative-law research, the presumptive sequence is:

1. identify the enabling statute;
2. identify delegated authority;
3. identify governing procedure;
4. locate Federal Register materials where applicable;
5. locate the agency docket;
6. identify the administrative record;
7. identify final agency action;
8. determine reviewability;
9. identify the governing standard of review;
10. identify available remedy;
11. preserve the research trail.

## 4. Contrary Authority

Researchers must identify material authority inconsistent with a preferred conclusion.

Contrary authority shall not be omitted merely because it weakens the argument.

## 5. Jurisdiction

Legal propositions must be evaluated within the appropriate jurisdiction.

Circuit-specific doctrine must be identified where material.

A rule from one jurisdiction shall not be represented as universally controlling unless authority supports that conclusion.

## 6. Primary-Source Verification

Where reasonably available, statutes, regulations, cases, agency materials, and public records should be verified against primary or official sources.

## 7. Research Trail

Material research should preserve sufficient information to allow another researcher to reproduce the analytical chain.

The research trail should identify:

- question presented;
- authorities reviewed;
- material contrary authority;
- source locations;
- quotations used;
- unresolved issues;
- verification limitations.

## 8. Institutional Candor

If a proposition cannot be verified, it must be:

- qualified;
- removed; or
- expressly flagged for further review.

Uncertainty shall not be hidden.

## 9. Capacity Discipline

Research must distinguish private individual capacity from representative, fiduciary, governmental, official, or institutional capacity where the distinction affects the legal analysis.

## Institutional Rule

**Authority Before Assertion.**
