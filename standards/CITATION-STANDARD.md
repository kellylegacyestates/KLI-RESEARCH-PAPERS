# Kelly Legacy Institute
## Citation Standard

**Repository:** KLI-RESEARCH-PAPERS  
**Institutional Owner:** Kelly Legacy Institute  
**Administrative Governance:** Office of the Fiduciary  

## 1. Purpose

This Standard governs citation practice for KLI research papers, preprints, briefs, memoranda, curriculum materials, and other controlled publications.

Citations must permit a reader to identify and, where reasonably possible, independently verify the authority supporting a material proposition.

## 2. General Rule

Material legal, factual, historical, technical, or institutional propositions shall be cited to the strongest reasonably available source.

Citation form must not create a false appearance of authority.

## 3. Cases

Case citations should identify:

- case name;
- reporter or authoritative database;
- court where not apparent;
- year;
- pinpoint citation where available and material.

A quotation must be checked against the actual opinion.

A syllabus or headnote shall not be represented as language of the court unless expressly identified as such.

## 4. Statutes

Statutory citations should identify:

- jurisdiction;
- title;
- section;
- subsection where material.

Current statutory text should be verified before publication where the proposition is date-sensitive.

## 5. Regulations

Regulatory citations should identify:

- title;
- Code of Federal Regulations part or section;
- subsection where material;
- current effective text where relevant.

Historical regulatory text should be clearly identified as historical.

## 6. Federal Register

Federal Register citations should identify, where available:

- volume;
- page;
- date;
- document type;
- agency;
- stable official source location.

Researchers should distinguish proposed rules, final rules, notices, corrections, and other publication types.

## 7. Administrative Records

Administrative-record citations should identify:

- agency;
- matter or proceeding;
- record identifier;
- page or item number;
- docket information where available.

Administrative-record pincites must not be invented.

If an administrative-record citation cannot be independently verified, the limitation must be disclosed or the citation omitted.

## 8. Agency Guidance and Decisions

Agency materials should identify:

- issuing agency or office;
- title;
- date;
- docket, decision number, or document number where available;
- source location.

Agency guidance must not be characterized as binding law unless authority supports that conclusion.

## 9. Government Reports and Official Records

Government materials should identify:

- issuing body;
- document title;
- publication date;
- report, docket, record, or document number where available;
- stable official source location.

## 10. Scholarly Sources

Secondary scholarship should identify:

- author;
- title;
- publication;
- volume;
- page;
- year.

Secondary authority should not substitute for primary authority on controlling legal propositions where primary authority is reasonably available.

## 11. Digital Sources

Digital citations should use stable official or institutional URLs where reasonably available.

Link rot risk should be considered for long-term institutional publications.

Where appropriate, preserve sufficient bibliographic information so the source remains identifiable even if a URL later changes.

## 12. Pinpoint Citations

A material doctrinal proposition should include a pinpoint citation where reasonably available.

The cited page or paragraph must actually support the proposition.

Approximate or decorative pincites are prohibited.

## 13. Quotations

Every direct quotation must be verified word-for-word.

Verify:

- wording;
- omissions;
- capitalization where material;
- punctuation where material;
- context;
- pincite.

Quotations should be used sparingly where paraphrase would preserve meaning more clearly.

## 14. Contrary Authority

Where a material legal conflict exists, citations should fairly identify relevant contrary authority.

Citation practice must not conceal a material split in authority.

## 15. Current Treatment

Where legal authority may have been limited, superseded, abrogated, or otherwise affected by later law, current treatment should be checked before publication.

A case should not be described as controlling merely because it has not been formally overruled.

## 16. Citation Integrity

Never fabricate:

- case citations;
- pincites;
- statutory subsections;
- docket numbers;
- quotations;
- Federal Register citations;
- administrative-record citations;
- source titles.

## Institutional Rule

**Verification Before Publication.**
